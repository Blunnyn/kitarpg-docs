Loot

A loot table says what a monster drops when it dies: an amount of gold and a list of items, each with its chance. This page covers every field of a loot table, how drops are rolled, quest drops, how loot reaches the player, and the settings and stats that change the odds.

#What a loot table is

A loot table is a LootTable asset in the Game Database. A monster points to one in its Loot field (loot). Several monsters can share one table: the sample's Slime and Giant Slime both use Slime Loot.

When a monster dies, the server rolls its table once for the player who earned the kill. Loot is only for that player: nobody else sees it or can take it.

#Where it is in the editor

Game Database > Items > Loot. The list shows every table; + New loot table makes one in a Loot folder next to the database and adds it to the database's lootTables list. You can also use Create > KitARPG > Loot Table in the Project window.

On a table's card, each drop is a row with the item's icon. The chance column shows Chance %: the data stores 0 to 1, the editor shows and takes percent.

#Fields

#The table

Field What it does Default
Id, Display Name, Description, Icon Common to every database asset. Only the id matters for loot tables. From the file name
Gold (gold) Minimum and maximum gold dropped (x and y, both included). 1 to 5
Drops (drops) The items it can drop, in order. Empty
Max Drops (maxDrops) Most items one kill can drop. 0 means no limit. 3

#Each drop

Field What it does Default
Item (item) The item. Project Check warns about a drop with no item. None
Chance (chance) Chance of dropping, shown as a percent (0 to 100). 0
Min Amount (minAmount) Fewest of the item when it drops. 0
Max Amount (maxAmount) Most of the item when it drops. 0
Quest (quest) Drops only for a killer on this quest who still needs the item. Empty: drops for anyone. None

The amount is a random whole number from Min Amount to Max Amount, both included, and never less than 1. A drop with both at 0 drops one.

#How a kill is rolled

When a monster dies, the server's Rewards component pays the player who dealt the most damage:

  1. Experience goes to the killer, or is split with party members nearby. See Monsters.
  2. Gold: a random amount from Gold, times the server's gold rate. A guild tax collector nearby takes its share first. See Tax collectors.
  3. Items: each drop is checked in list order:
    1. A drop with a Quest is skipped unless the killer has that quest open and carries fewer of the item than its collect objective asks for.
    2. A random roll against the drop's chance (see Odds). On a hit, the item and its amount are added.
    3. When the number of items added reaches Max Drops, the rest of the list isn't rolled.
  4. Delivery: the gold and items reach the player the way Game Settings > Loot says (see How loot reaches the player).

Because the list stops at Max Drops, order matters. Put drops you never want cut first. The sample puts its quest drops at the top of Slime Loot and Mushroom Loot for this reason.

#Odds

For each item that isn't a quest drop, the chance used is:

chance x (1 + the killer's Loot Chance stat) x the server's loot rate, capped at 100%.

  • Loot Chance (lootChance) is a stat. 0.1 is +10% of each chance, not +10 points: a 5% drop becomes 5.5%. In the sample, each point of Luck gives +0.5%.
  • The loot rate is Game Settings > Rates > Loot Rate (lootRate, default 1) times a rates event a GM may have started with /rates loot 2 60.
  • Quest drops keep their own chance. Luck and rates never make a quest easier.

Gold uses Gold Rate (goldRate, default 1) the same way. See Game settings and GM commands.

#How loot reaches the player

Game Settings > Loot has two settings (GameInstance, on the KitNetwork prefab):

Field What it does Default
Loot Mode (lootMode) How players get what monsters drop. Only the player who earned the kill sees it. Ground
Auto Gold (autoGold) Gold goes straight to the player. Off: it drops like the items, on the ground or in the body. On

The loot modes:

Mode What the player sees
Ground Each item falls on the ground next to the monster, scattered within about 1.2 m, as its icon with its name in its rarity color. Clicking one walks there and picks it up. Gold (when Auto Gold is off) falls as a pile.
Corpse Items stay in the monster's body, which lies grey where it died. Clicking it opens the loot window, to take items one by one or all.
Automatic Items go straight to the bag. What doesn't fit falls on the ground.

Details that apply in every mode:

  • Loot waits 90 seconds, then disappears. It also disappears when its owner leaves the map.
  • The player picks it up within 2 m (a little more while the loot window is open).
  • Loot may put the bag over its weight limit. The character then can't walk until it drops, sells or stores something. See Inventory and bank.
  • Gold that doesn't fit in the purse goes to the bank.
  • Items of Game Settings > Logs > Log Loot From rarity or better (Rare by default) are written to the game log. See Game log and reports.

Loot never exists as a network object. The server keeps it in memory and tells only its owner where it is. Another client, even a modified one, can't see it or pick it up.

#Build a loot table

This builds a table for a wolf, with a quest drop.

  1. Game Database > Items > Loot > + New loot table. Rename it Wolf Loot.
  2. Set Gold to 2 and 7.
  3. Set Max Drops to 2.
  4. Add the quest drop first: Item Wolf Pelt, Chance % 60, Min Amount 1, Max Amount 1, Quest "Pelts for the Tanner".
  5. Add the normal drops: Health Potion at 25%, 1 to 1; a rune at 15%, 1 to 2; a rare cloak at 2%, 1 to 1.
  6. Open World > Monsters > Wolf and pick Wolf Loot in Loot.
  7. Make the quest: a CollectObjective for 5 Wolf Pelts. Mark the pelt as a quest item (Items > Quest items makes it so). See Quests.
  8. Test: /spawn wolf 5, kill them with the quest open and without it. Without the quest, no pelts drop.
Tip

A boss with guaranteed drops: give those items 100%, and raise Max Drops so the other drops still have room. The sample's Mushroom King always drops Holy Water and has Max Drops 5.

#The bestiary

The bestiary (B) lists each monster's drops with the chance the player has, including their loot stat. Turn a monster's In Bestiary off to hide it there.

#Single player and MMO

The rolls are the same in both. In MMO they happen on the map server where the monster died, and only the owner's client is told about the loot. In a single player game they happen on the host. In MMO, every item in the player's bag is a row in the database with one owner; loot becomes such a row when it is picked up.

#For programmers

Rewards.OnKilled, Rewards.DropChance and Rewards.ShareExperience are virtual. Override DropChance to change the odds rule, or OnKilled to share loot with a party. Rewards.Instance.Pay(...) hands items and gold to a player following the loot mode, for loot of your own. See Overriding kit behaviour.

#Common mistakes

  • A drop never falls. Its chance is 0, Max Drops is reached by drops above it, or it has a Quest the killer doesn't have open.
  • A quest item stops dropping. The player already carries as many as the objective asks for. This is on purpose.
  • Drops seem too generous. The loot rate in Game Settings, a GM rates event, or a high Loot Chance stat. Check /rates.
  • The party wonders why only one got the loot. Loot goes to the top damage dealer. Experience and quest kill counts are shared; items aren't.
