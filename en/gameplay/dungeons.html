Gameplay systems

Dungeons

An instanced map, or dungeon, gives each party, or each player alone, its own copy of the map, with its own monsters and boss. This page covers how copies work for the player and on the server, every setting, and how to make a dungeon step by step.

#For the player

A dungeon looks like any other map. You walk through its portal (in the sample, west of the forest ruins) and arrive at its spawn point. What changes is who else is there:

  • In a party, you and your party members share one copy, even if you go in at different times.
  • Alone, you get a copy of your own.

Other players never see your copy and you never see theirs. Monsters, bosses and loot in your copy are yours.

When everyone has left, the copy stays for a while (5 minutes by default) so you can come back to it, for example after dying. After that it closes, and the next time you enter you get a fresh one.

When you log in again inside a dungeon, you appear at the copy's spawn point, never at your saved position.

#Settings

Each map's dungeon settings are in the Game Database (Maps), section Instances.

Field What it does Default
Instanced (instanced) Each party, or each player alone, gets its own copy. Online only Off
Instance Spacing (instanceSpacing) Meters between copies on the map server, so they never see each other 1,000
Empty Minutes (emptyMinutes) Minutes a copy stays after its last player leaves 5

#How it works

#Which copy you get

Central picks the copy when it issues the ticket to enter the map: party-<id> for players in a party and solo-<id> for players alone. The copy goes with the ticket and the map server puts the player there. It is never saved with the character.

#On the map server

The map server runs every copy of the map in one process:

  • The scene it starts with is only the template. Its spawners stay idle, so it has no monsters.
  • Each copy is the same scene loaded again and moved index × Instance Spacing meters along X. Copies are far apart, so the interest grid, line of sight and monster AI need nothing special.
  • Each copy gets its own NavMesh, made from the template's data.
  • Monsters stay in the scene of the spawner that made them.
  • A player always enters at their copy's SpawnPoint.
  • A copy closes Empty Minutes after it empties: its network objects are destroyed, its NavMesh removed and the scene unloaded.

#On the client

The client gets the copy's offset before its character appears and moves its own scene by the same amount, so positions match. Clients don't use the NavMesh.

#Step by step: make a dungeon

  1. Make the scene. Duplicate a sample map such as Forest, or make your own with a SpawnPoint and a baked NavMeshSurface. Add MonsterSpawners and a boss. Underground maps usually leave out the DayNightCycle (the sample's crypt has none).
  2. Add the map. Game Database > Maps > New map, pick the scene in Scene and turn on Instanced. Set Empty Minutes to how long players may take to come back.
  3. Connect it. In the map players come from, add a Portal whose Target Map is the dungeon's id and Target Position is inside it. In the dungeon, add a portal back. See Maps.
  4. Fill the bestiary. On the map's card, press Read from scene so the bestiary knows its monsters.
  5. Register the map with Central. Each map runs as its own map server on its own port. On your computer, Play & Servers gives every map of the database a port when it starts the services. On a server, add the map's id and a free port to Central's Maps setting and to Docker Compose. See Launcher and Docker.
  6. Rebuild the map server. Builds take every map's scene from the Game Database. Play & Servers warns when the build lacks a map's scene.
  7. Try it in MMO with two players: in a party they share a copy; alone they don't. See Play & Servers.

The sample's Sunken Crypt (crypt, port 7779) has rooms with pillars, slimes, mushrooms and the Giant Slime at the far end.

#Single player and MMO

Single player MMO
Copies None: an instanced map is an ordinary map, because a single player world is one copy anyway One per party or solo player
Empty Minutes Not used Yes

Copies only exist on a dedicated map server. In the editor, run the map as a server to try copies (Play & Servers).

#Guild halls

Guild halls use the same system: the hall map is instanced and each guild that bought one gets the copy guild-<id>. Central only lets members of that guild (and of allied guilds, if allowed) into it.

#Tips

  • Give a dungeon an Empty Minutes long enough for players to run back from the spawn point after dying.
  • Keep Instance Spacing well above the size of the map. The default 1,000 m fits any normal map; raise it for maps larger than that.
  • Bosses with phases and guards work in copies like anywhere else. See Bosses.

#Common mistakes

  • Players end up back at the login when they enter. The map isn't registered with Central, or the map server build doesn't have its scene. Check Play & Servers and rebuild.
  • The party lands in different copies. Someone entered before joining the party. Central picks the copy when the ticket is issued: form the party first, then enter.
  • Everyone shares the dungeon. Instanced is off, or you are testing in single player.
  • The template has monsters. Put monsters in a dungeon with MonsterSpawners. The template scene's spawners stay idle and each copy's spawners fill that copy.
