KitARPG.Modes.ThirdPerson

#Blunnyn.KitARPG.Modes.ThirdPerson

#ThirdPersonCamera (class)

Camera behind the local player for the third person and free camera modes. Third person hides the cursor and turns with the mouse, aiming with a crosshair beside the player; a window, a dialog or holding Alt gives the cursor back. Free camera keeps the cursor and turns while the right button is held. The wheel zooms within the allowed range, and walls between the camera and the player pull it in. The framing is the game's, from CameraSettings: players only choose mouse sensitivity and inversion.

Member Description
public CameraSettings settings Empty: the one in Game Settings for this mode.
public LayerMask blockers What the camera can't go through.
public int lookButton Free camera: mouse button held to look around. 0 left, 1 right, 2 middle.
public KeyCode freeCursorKey Third person: held to get the cursor back.
public float yaw Where the camera faces, in degrees around the player.
public bool Looking The mouse turns the camera now and the cursor is hidden.
public static bool Crosshair Third person aims with a crosshair; free camera with the mouse.
public static bool TurnsPlayer Third person with Settings > Controls > Turn with the camera on: the player faces where the camera looks.
public CameraSettings Settings The framing in use: this camera's own, the mode's in Game Settings, or the kit's defaults.

#ThirdPersonInput (class)

Client half of the third person and free camera modes. WASD or the arrows walk relative to the camera, Space dodges, F picks up or talks to the nearest thing. Aiming is free: the left button swings toward the aim (the crosshair in third person, the mouse in free camera) and keeps swinging while held, and skills go there too; nothing needs a target, the server finds who is hit. The player faces where it walks or strikes; in third person it can turn with the camera instead (Settings > Controls). A right click that doesn't turn the camera opens the menu on another player. With TouchControls, the stick walks, dragging on the world turns the camera, Attack swings at what the crosshair covers or the nearest enemy, and a tap on the world picks up, talks or opens another player's menu. The player moves at once: each tick is sent to the server and replayed over its answer, so the client only corrects itself when the server disagrees. A host just sends its keys.

Member Description
public float interactRange Meters around the player in which F finds loot or an NPC.
public float swingRepeat Seconds between swings while the button is held. The server keeps the real attack speed.
public float faceAimSeconds Seconds the player keeps facing what it attacked or cast at, even while walking another way.
public float snapDistance Meters off the server at which the player jumps there instead of easing over.
public float correctionSharpness How fast a small correction eases out.
